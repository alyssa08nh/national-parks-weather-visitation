A decade in the wild · 2007—2016

When the forecast becomes part of the itinerary

Thousands of monthly visits to 60 US national parks, set beside the temperatures and precipitation that shape a day outside. Explore the broad pattern, then step inside one park at a time.

The national picture

Each point is one park-month. The warm band is busy: visitation rises as outdoor conditions become more comfortable, but the relationship is not a straight line. Desert parks often trade summer heat for winter crowds, while high-latitude parks compress their season into a few bright months.

Code 
overview <- parks |>
  mutate(year = factor(year))

plot_ly(
  overview,
  x = ~average_temperature_f,
  y = ~visitor_count,
  type = "scatter",
  mode = "markers",
  color = ~year,
  colors = "YlOrRd",
  size = ~precipitation_in,
  sizes = c(6, 28),
  opacity = .55,
  text = ~paste(park_name, month_name, year, sep = " · "),
  hovertemplate = "%{text}<br>%{x:.0f}°F · %{y:,.0f} visits<extra></extra>"
) |>
  layout(
    xaxis = list(title = "Average monthly temperature (°F)", zeroline = FALSE),
    yaxis = list(title = "Monthly visitor count", tickformat = ","),
    legend = list(title = list(text = "Year"))
  )

Data note: annual visitation totals come from the National Park Service Stats archive via TidyTuesday. Monthly counts and weather values are a reproducible teaching approximation derived from annual totals and regional climate profiles; see the data-preparation script for the method.
